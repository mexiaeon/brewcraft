How to choose an espresso grinder

1. Match the grinder to your machine and basket

If your espresso machine uses a pressurized basket, you may have more flexibility with grind quality. For a non-pressurized basket, fine adjustment and consistency matter much more. If you own a Breville Bambino, the Encore ESP is a sensible entry point; more advanced users may prefer a stepless single-dose grinder.

2. Hopper or single dose?

A hopper grinder stores beans and is convenient when you use the same coffee every day. A single-dose grinder asks you to weigh beans for each shot, but makes it easier to switch coffees and keep only the dose you need in the grinder.

3. Stepped or stepless adjustment?

Stepped grinders click between set positions. They are easy to understand, but sometimes the ideal espresso setting sits between two clicks. Stepless grinders allow finer adjustment, which can help when dialing in espresso, but they may take more practice.

4. Do not buy by burr size alone

Larger burrs can improve speed and change the grinder's character, but burr size alone does not guarantee better espresso. Adjustment range, grind consistency, retention, workflow, service and the coffee you enjoy all matter.

5. Leave room in the budget for the rest of the setup

A scale, fresh beans and a good routine can matter more than jumping to a much more expensive grinder. If your total budget is limited, choose a capable grinder and keep some money for a scale and basic accessories.
