How to choose the right machine

1. Decide how you drink coffee

If you mostly drink straight espresso or Americanos, manual milk steaming may not matter. If you make cappuccinos and lattes every day, the Bambino Plus automatic milk feature can be worth paying for.

2. Set aside money for a grinder

For espresso made from fresh beans, choose a grinder designed for espresso adjustment. A machine cannot compensate for a grinder that cannot produce a consistent, adjustable fine grind.

3. Think about workflow and learning

Automatic milk texturing reduces one learning curve. A manual wand gives you more practice and control. Neither is automatically better; it depends on whether you enjoy the process or want a faster routine.

4. Check the exact model and included accessories

Retailer bundles, regional model numbers, filter baskets and finishes can differ. Confirm the model number, voltage, warranty, return policy and included accessories before checkout.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Breville Bambino Plus really under $500?

Breville lists it at $499.95 in the US at the time of this research, before tax. That leaves almost no room in a $500 total budget for a grinder, so compare sale prices and account for the full setup cost.

Do I need a separate grinder?

For the best control with fresh beans, yes. None of the four machines in this guide includes a built-in burr grinder. A dedicated espresso-capable grinder is a separate purchase.

Is a 15-bar pump the same as 15-bar espresso?

No. Pump pressure ratings are not the same as the pressure at the coffee puck during extraction. Focus on the machine's brewing design, basket type, grinder and your ability to dial in the shot rather than treating a higher advertised pump number as a quality score.

Are these machines tested by Brewcraft?

No. This guide compares manufacturer-published information and reference pricing. Brewcraft has not physically tested these machines, and we do not present this as a lab test or hands-on ranking.
