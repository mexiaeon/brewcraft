How to choose an espresso grinder

1. Prioritize adjustment, not just burr size

Espresso needs small adjustments because even a minor grind change can speed up or slow down extraction significantly. A stepless grinder lets you move between points; a stepped grinder can be simpler but offers fixed positions.

2. Choose between a hopper and single dosing

A hopper is convenient if you make coffee every day with the same beans. Single dosing means weighing beans for each brew, but makes it easier to switch coffees and control exactly how much goes into the grinder.

3. Match electric or manual to your routine

An electric grinder is more convenient for several drinks a day. A manual grinder saves counter space, needs no electricity and is usually quieter, but takes effort for every dose.

4. Leave room in the budget for basic accessories

A good grinder is only part of the workflow. Consider a small scale, a tamper that fits your portafilter and a distribution tool if needed. Do not spend the entire budget on the grinder.

Frequently asked questions

Can a grinder under $300 make good espresso?

Yes. The models in this guide are designed to grind fine enough for espresso and provide usable adjustments. How easy they are to dial in depends on the grinder, beans and espresso machine.

Are flat burrs always better than conical burrs?

No. Burr type influences the grinding profile, but it does not determine quality on its own. Adjustment precision, consistency, design and workflow matter too.

Do I need a separate grinder if my espresso machine has one built in?

Not necessarily. If the built-in grinder can make sufficiently fine and consistent adjustments, you can start with it. Consider a separate grinder if you need more control or want to improve the workflow.
